# Tone and horror rules

## Feeling
Quiet dread with a dry wit. The player is alone in a video shop with something that exists only on tape. The scare is the lens: raising it and finding the Thing already close. We do not do jump-scare corridors.

## Rules
- **Show less.** The Thing is seen only on the lens, and then briefly. Withholding is stronger than showing.
- **The reveal is the payoff.** Anything that would show the Thing without the lens belongs to the end replay, not to play.
- **Silence is a tool.** After a loud moment, let it go quiet. After a tape ends, the shop is quiet and something in it has changed.
- **The narrator is deadpan.** It is a corporate training voice that stays cheerful while the situation is not. It never explains the horror. Few words, never a paragraph.
- **Guide text is a few words that fade** once the player does the thing on purpose. Prefer showing: a wire, a lamp, a footprint. All-caps sentences are a smell.
- **Material:** plastic, magnetic tape, lino, rain, fluorescent tube, CRT glass. Each tape adds one material of its own.
- **Palette:** near-black, fluorescent green-white, REC red (the accent), tape-blue. Each tape adds one accent colour.

## Placeholders
The title, setting, story, the Thing's nature, the narrator's lines and the tape names are the author's. Anything written here is marked ⟨placeholder⟩ in the design and listed in `docs/placeholders.md`. Do not invent lore.
